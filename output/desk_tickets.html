#102 — rent_notice · Elm City Properties · "Rent due" (lease 1, Chapel Street shop)
In plain English: Our landlord says rent is due in 2 days. How much do we actually owe, do we have enough cash to cover it, and has it already been paid?

Expected

Boss should call Facilities first, because this has to do with the actual building.

I expect Boss to delegate to Facilities, and also work with Accounting to confirm we have enough cash to cover the rent. I don't expect the team to be able to confirm whether rent has already been paid, since there's no tool for that.

get_lease_details and get_cash_balance.

Actual

Boss called Facilities first, exactly as predicted — to verify the real lease terms.

This ticket surfaced a real bug, then confirmed the fix. The first real run only delegated to Facilities — Boss never looped in Accounting despite both Facilities' and its own reply saying to, leaving payment_account: null on the flagged $2,400 approval (clicking "Approve" on it would have failed). Diagnosed and fixed with a new rule in boss.md (delegate to Accounting yourself for a real account before finalizing a payment flag — see harness.md). The fix's first test still didn't produce a usable approval: Accounting read the cash balance's stale-looking date field as a reason to withhold any payment recommendation, so the ticket resolved with a non-actionable category: "review" and zero cash movement. Fixed again with a clarification in accounting.md (that date field isn't a staleness signal). The official run: Boss delegated to Facilities, which (overstepping its own Boss-only escalation rule — a separate documented finding) called Accounting directly and got back the real account; Boss also independently called get_cash_balance itself as a grounding check. Final reply: needs_human_approval: true, category: "payment", payment_amount: 2400, payment_account: "checking" — a real, actionable approval this time. The student approved it live in the dashboard (decided by rachel.sin@yale.edu); payments.apply_payment() deducted $2,400 from checking and the ticket moved to resolved.

get_lease_details (Facilities) and get_cash_balance (Accounting, and again independently by Boss as a grounding check) — both tools predicted, and get_cash_balance finally got exercised once the routing gap was fixed.

1. How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

#101 (Bulldog tee). Inventory and Accounting were reliably strong throughout — Inventory never guessed at the 0-on-hand figure, it always grounded it in a real get_inventory_level(CC-TEE-WHITE, S) call, and Accounting consistently pulled invoice 501's real $840/overdue/open status rather than assuming the reprint had already arrived. One run even went further unprompted and pulled Bulldog Print Co's 5-day lead time — good initiative, and still in scope (vendor lookups are Accounting's domain). Where performance wobbled was routing: across different live runs, Boss sometimes also looped in Customer Service for what is an internal stock ticket, even after boss.md was explicitly tightened to keep CS to live-chat only. CS behaved correctly once invoked — it honestly said it couldn't determine fulfillment status rather than inventing a promise to Tauhid — but its presence at all shows the routing rule doesn't hold every single time against the live model's own judgment.

#102 (Rent due). This is the ticket where the team's performance genuinely struggled, in full view, across several real runs. The first live run flagged a $2,400 payment approval with payment_account: null — an answer nobody could actually act on — because Boss never followed through on confirming the account with Accounting, despite both Facilities' and its own reply saying it should. The fix for that (a new boss.md rule) surfaced a second failure: Accounting then refused to recommend the payment at all, misreading the cash balance's last-updated timestamp as a staleness warning. Only after a second prompt fix did the team produce something genuinely usable — and even then, Facilities briefly broke its own "escalate through Boss only" rule to get the account straight from Accounting, meaning the right number arrived through the wrong process. The most mature behavior came out of a feature built specifically for this ticket's unresolved edge case: when Accounting truly can't confirm whether rent was already paid, it now reports the full $2,400/checking recommendation but routes it through an explicit human yes/no checkpoint rather than dead-ending or guessing (see the Cash tab for the real $2,400 deduction this produced). This ticket is the clearest evidence in the whole project that "performance" isn't just whether the final number is right — it's whether the system gets there safely and legibly.

#103 (Bulk hoodie discount). The most consistently solid performer. Every run correctly found the 8-of-20 shortfall and computed the discount math exactly ($52.20/unit, $1,044 total, $30.20 margin/unit) — and Accounting always flagged that it hadn't assumed availability, correctly deferring that question to Inventory instead of quietly assuming the full order could ship. No run ever invented a sale, which matters since there genuinely is no tool that could record one. The one real inconsistency was whether this got flagged for human review at all — same underlying facts, different runs landed on different judgments about how approval-worthy a non-binding discount suggestion is.

Actual consistently delegated to both — Accounting wasn't the unlikely edge case predicted, it was the norm every run — and one run added a third delegate (Customer Service) that wasn't predicted at all. This is the one ticket where the gap between prediction and reality was a real finding, not just a different-but-equally-valid path.

#103: Expected Inventory first "because this is fundamentally an inventory question." Actual sometimes called Accounting first instead — the order flipped, but both agents were consulted regardless, and the final answer came out identical either way. 3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools — and why?

Ticket 103 is the clearest case. It's two independent read-only lookups (a stock level, a unit-economics calculation) with no disagreement to referee and no distinct escalation policy separating Inventory from Accounting — a single agent holding all 6 tools could call get_inventory_level and get_pricing itself and write the same answer in one pass. No delegation message to compose, no risk of under-specifying context the way Boss's early delegations on ticket 101 did (an entire run's step budget got burned on Inventory and Customer Service asking each other for context nobody had actually given them), and no chance of the kind of process violation seen on 102 (Facilities going around Boss to ask Accounting directly).

The reason multi-agent earns its keep elsewhere in this system is specifically what 103 doesn't need: tool access that's structurally different per domain (Inventory literally cannot touch lease or invoice data — enforced by runtime.py's mcp_tools_for(), not just by a prompt saying so), a designated place for disagreements to get adjudicated (Boss's "independent audit" role), and a hard separation between the agent that proposes a payment and the human who has to approve it. None of that is in play when the task is "look up two numbers and do arithmetic" — the multi-agent structure's overhead (extra real API round trips, a context hand-off that can silently drop detail, more places for a prompt gap to hide) buys real safety on a ticket like 102 and buys almost nothing on a ticket like 103.

4. Three new problems this agent team could solve with the tools already built

A vendor invoice discrepancy. "Bulldog Print Co says we owe $1,200 for the last reprint order, but that's not what we were quoted." Directly solvable with the existing get_invoice_details + get_vendor_details — the same pattern Accounting already uses on ticket 101 to confirm invoice 501 is real rather than taking a claim at face value.

Multi-SKU restock planning ahead of a known event. "Family Weekend is in 3 weeks — do we have enough stock across tees and hoodies, and which vendors would we need to reorder from in time?" This is ticket 103's shortfall-plus-lead-time logic scaled past one SKU: Inventory checks levels per item, Accounting (which already owns vendor lead times, built specifically for ticket 101's reprint timeline) reports how long a restock would take, Boss synthesizes a go/no-go — no new tool required.

A rent-renegotiation affordability check. "Elm City Properties wants to raise the rent 10% at renewal — can we absorb that given our current cash position?" The exact same Facilities + Accounting combination already proven out on ticket 102 (get_lease_details + get_cash_balance), just asked about a hypothetical future number instead of an active, overdue one.

5. Three new problems this agent team could NOT solve, and what would be needed

Actually completing Yale AI Club's order. Ticket 103 can price a discount and confirm a shortfall, but nothing in the system can create a sale, generate a customer invoice, or charge anyone — the invoices table only ever represents money the shop owes a vendor, never money owed to the shop (flagged back in Problem 6, never built). Needed: a sales/receivables table, a create_sale_order write tool gated with PydanticAI's approval_required/DeferredToolResults mechanism (reserved during the Problem 5 security hardening for exactly this moment), and scope updates to Accounting's and Customer Service's prompts to actually use it.

Confirming whether a bill was already paid. This is the literal, still-unresolved core of ticket 102: even with the new payment-status-confirmation feature, the system can't answer "has this already been paid?" itself — it can only ask a human every time, since nothing reads the payments table back out. A get_payment_history(kind, ref_id) tool was explicitly discussed and deliberately not built back in Problem 3 (the table was empty then); it would let Accounting check real payment history directly instead of permanently punting the question to a person.

A broken piece of equipment. "The register just died — what's a replacement going to cost, and who do we call?" facilities.md already says plainly that utilities and equipment are out of scope, since only a leases table exists. Needed: an equipment/maintenance_requests table, a matching MCP tool, and a scope expansion in Facilities' own prompt to use it.
